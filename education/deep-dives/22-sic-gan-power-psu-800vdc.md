## 22-S01 Converters are not magic: η and heat
Power flows utility/grid AC → certified switchgear+UPS and rectifier/PSU → rack bus/DC converter → board VRM → low-voltage GPU/CPU rail. Actual placement and architecture vary with 800VDC designs. Efficiency η = Pout/Pin; input Pin=Pout/η, converter loss=Pin−Pout. In **fictional one-stage 48V DC-input → downstream 100W DC** load, η=0.90 means Pin111.111111W and loss11.111111W; η=.95 means Pin105.263158W and loss5.263158W. Difference in local converter loss ≈5.847953W, NOT site power saved and not load change. Input nominal current under fixed48V 2.3148148A vs2.1929825A; no transient harmonics or thermal-feedback load modeled. Cascaded stages efficiency is product of individual efficiencies only at those specific conditions; fixed efficiency independent of power is toy. PUE facility overhead is separate (Lesson31).

## 22-S02 Material physics, gate drive and capacitive switching energy
Infineon wide-bandgap overview [R1], GaN FET [R2], Wolfspeed SiC drivers [R3][R4] identify electric-field breakdown, switch/drive, cost, thermal, VGS and reliability tradeoffs. SiC MOSFETs often serve higher bus voltages/power systems and require rated driver design; power GaN HEMTs often serve high-frequency dense PSU designs but GaN-on-Si and RF GaN-on-SiC **are not the same application or component**. No material determines a fixed universal loss/current/frequency, and a 1200V/650V rated PART must be chosen using actual data sheet, margin, insulation and standards, never label material as an absolute device rating. SiC gate drivers require negative/positive VGS specifics per vendor; e-mode GaN gate drive differs. Do not expose physical hookup or gate-bias build recipe.
Toy switching **voltage-independent lumped fixed Ceff only**: assume 1 charge and 1 discharge of *the same effective capacitor* every period. Energy drawn per charge Echarge=C V² (stored C V²/2 and dissipated C V²/2 on ideal charge, then stored is dissipated on discharge, ignoring recovery): `PcapToy=Ceff*V²*f`. For Ceff=100pF, V=100V and f100kHz =>0.1W; f500kHz=>0.5W; V200V and f100kHz=>0.4W; f500kHz=>2.0W. This is not total MOSFET Eoss/Eon/Eoff, real device C nonlinear with voltage, ZVS can recover energy and circuit topology alters hard-switch loss. Independently adjust V100|200 and f100k|500k. ## 22-S03 Buck inductor ripple, switching frequency and EMI
Ideal steady-state CCM buck D≈Vout/Vin; during ON inductor voltage Vin−Vout, rise ΔI=(Vin−Vout)*D/(L*f). Hypothetical `Vin=48V`, `Vout=12V`, D=.25, load average30A ensures triangular ripple9A remains above zero. L10µH/f100kHz => (36*.25)/(10e−6*100e3)=9A p-p; L10µH/f200kHz=>4.5A; L20µH/f100kHz=>4.5A; L20µH/f200kHz=>2.25A. More f reduces ripple at fixed L and ideal D but can raise switching-related losses, EMI/common-mode currents, driver heating; larger L may cost size/core/copper losses. Topology, core saturation, nonideal deadtime and device EMI compliance NOT measured. This is generic power-stage abstraction, NOT a validated 48V server design or physical construction.

## 22-S04 Joule heat, junction temperature and lifetime uncertainty
Thermal steady-state first-order `Tj = Ta+ Ploss*RθJA` assumes lumped resistance, steady cooling boundary, no thermal coupling. With `Ta=30°C`, `lossW=20|40`, `thermalResistanceKperW=1|2`: 20*1=>50°C,20*2=>70°C,40*1=>70°C,40*2=>110°C. Fictional classroom `alertAtTj=100°C` =>110 flagged INVESTIGATE, no product max junction claim. SiC and GaN don't eliminate losses/heat and 110°C does not imply a specific real device failed. Wolfspeed SiC VGS/TDDB caveats [R3][R4][R5].

## 22-S05 400 vs 800VDC distribution current, I²R heat and protection
NVIDIA published May2025 800VDC architecture direction and Aug 11 2026 update [R6][R7], with OCP ecosystems. Distinguish announced roadmap/design/reference from universal deployed facility conversion or measured site 50% energy reduction. Hypothetical nominal one DC distribution route carrying **source-side rated power P=80kW** with **equal lumped conductor loop resistance R=.05Ω** at 400V vs800V. Simplified I=P/V =>400V:200A,800V:100A. Path drop I*R=>10V vs5V. I²R loss=>2000W vs500W, i.e. **single resistive route only** 75% lower loss. At these ideal source-side fixed 80kW powers, receiver path power after wire loss 78kW vs79.5kW, so this comparison is NOT fixed80kW received load. If the latter is the requirement, currents must solve `Pdelivered=(Vsource-I R)*I` and differ; do not conflate. Real converters/UPS/PDU/busway/grounding/leakage/arc risks/creepage-clearance/fault isolation codes matter. Higher voltage increases safety hazards and needs specially designed insulation, certified components and qualified staff. No physical live bus assembly or personal test instructions.
